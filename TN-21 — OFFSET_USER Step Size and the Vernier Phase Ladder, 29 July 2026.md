## 1. The result in one line

**[measured]** One OFFSET_USER register step is

$$s = 0.499513 \pm 0.000073\ \Delta/\text{step} = 30.4879\ \text{mdps/step},$$

where $\Delta = 2000/32768\ \text{dps} = 61.0352$ mdps is the 16-bit register LSB and the uncertainty is the between-axis scatter of three independent replicates.

The datasheet figure of $1/32$ dps $= 0.512\,\Delta$ is **excluded at 338 σ**. It is wrong by 2.4%.

## 2. What was run

`plan_offset.txt`, slot 1, ODR 1000, AAF default, nine 60 s records:

```
k = 0, 1, 20, 0, 125, 1000, 0, 2000, 0
```

The four returns to $k = 0$ are the design's whole point. They measure the baseline nuisance directly, whatever its cause, so the step size does not have to be separated from a drift model that might be wrong — and §5 shows the drift model *was* wrong.

Measured shifts, baseline removed by interpolating between the two flanking brackets:

| $k$ | shift (Δ) | $\hat s = \text{shift}/k$ | bias from $c$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.4949 | 0.494868 | −0.036414 |
| 20 | 9.9290 | 0.496449 | −0.001821 |
| 125 | 62.4147 | 0.499318 | −0.000291 |
| 1000 | 499.5043 | 0.499504 | −0.000036 |
| 2000 | 999.1021 | 0.499551 | −0.000018 |

The estimates converge upward with $k$. That is not a nonlinearity in the register — it is a **fixed additive error per record**, so the shift is fitted as

$$\text{shift}(k) = s\,k + c$$

with $c$ absorbing settling and the bracket's own interpolation error. Reading $\hat s$ off a single pair, which is exactly what the 28 July trio did at $k = 4$, folds $c$ into $s$ and biases it by $c/k$. At $k = 4$ that bias is $\approx 0.01\,\Delta/\text{step}$ — **larger than the 0.012 separation between the two hypotheses under test.** The original measurement could not have worked at any sample size.

## 3. The consequence, and it inverts TN-20 §2.5

TN-20 §2.5 feared that $s = 0.500$ exactly would make the ladder **degenerate**: even step counts landing on $\varphi = 0$, odd on $\varphi = 0.5$, two reachable phases out of the 125 the arithmetic promised, and the digital phase axis dead.

$s$ is not 0.500. It is $1/2 - \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon = 4.87\times10^{-4}$, and that changes the character of the register completely.

**[inference]** Write $\varphi_k = \operatorname{frac}(sk)$. With $s = 1/2 - \varepsilon$:

- even steps $k = 2m$ give $\varphi = \operatorname{frac}(-2m\varepsilon)$ — a comb that **precesses by $2\varepsilon$ per step**
- odd steps give the same comb offset by $\approx 1/2$

The full period is traversed in $1/(2\varepsilon) = 1026$ steps, and the register holds 2047. So over its range the register reaches **2048 distinct phases with a largest gap of 0.00097 Δ**.

> **The ladder is a vernier, not a degenerate pair — and it is 8× finer than the 64/125 scheme the corpus assumed** (125 phases at 0.008 Δ spacing).

Step counts for a uniform eight-point ladder, from the measured $s$:

| target $\varphi$ | $k$ | reaches |
|---|---|---|
| 0.000 | 0 | 0.0000 |
| 0.125 | 1796 | 0.1252 |
| 0.250 | 1540 | 0.2499 |
| 0.375 | 257 | 0.3748 |
| 0.500 | 1026 | 0.5003 |
| 0.625 | 770 | 0.6250 |
| 0.750 | 514 | 0.7496 |
| 0.875 | 1283 | 0.8751 |

Generated by `phase_ladder()` in `offset_fit.py`, so the table regenerates from whatever $s$ the next specimen measures.

**Caveat that must travel with this table. [inference]** The vernier depends on $\varepsilon$, which is a small difference between two much larger numbers and is a *per-axis, per-part* quantity (§4). Every specimen needs its own ladder calibrated before its own ladder is run. Ten minutes per part, and `plan_offset.txt` already does it.

## 4. Why $s \approx 1/2$, and why not exactly

**[inference]** $0.512\,\Delta$ is $8.192$ codes of the 20-bit field ($\Delta = 16$ codes, TN-20 §4.4). That is not an integer, so if the offset were summed at the 20-bit word the step would have to be a whole number of codes — 8, i.e. exactly $0.500\,\Delta$.

A natural mechanism gives exactly that: if the offset register's $1/32$ dps is referenced internally to a **±2048 dps** full scale — a power of two, which is what silicon would do — rather than the ±2000 dps of the specification table, then

$$s_{\text{nominal}} = \frac{1/32}{2048/32768} = 0.5\ \Delta\ \text{exactly.}$$

The measured value sits 0.097% below that. **[inference]** The natural candidate for the residual is the per-axis sensitivity trim: the datasheet quotes ±0.5% initial tolerance on scale factor, and the three axes here differ by 0.026% — the same order, and in the right pattern for an offset injected *upstream* of a per-axis gain. If so, $s_\text{axis} = 0.5\,g_\text{axis}$ and this run measures slot 1's per-axis gains to one part in $10^4$.

That is a hypothesis, not a finding. **[verify]** It predicts that (a) slot 2 gives a different $\varepsilon$, and (b) $\varepsilon$ correlates with each axis's measured sensitivity. Both are cheap: (a) is the commented-out block at the foot of `plan_offset.txt`; (b) needs a rate table this project does not have.

## 5. Two things confirmed for free

**5.1 The offset is applied upstream of the 16-bit truncation. [measured]**

The shift measured on the 19-bit stream and on the 16-bit register agree to better than 0.0015 Δ at every $k$ up to 2000:

| $k$ | $\delta\mu_{20}$ | $\delta\mu_{16}$ | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.5631 | 0.5645 | −0.00149 |
| 125 | 62.5524 | 62.5533 | −0.00088 |
| 1000 | 499.6595 | 499.6607 | −0.00117 |
| 2000 | 999.0768 | 999.0762 | +0.00060 |

This is TN-13 §4.3's pre-register placement premise, which the corpus has carried as an assumption since the ladder was designed. It is now measured.

**5.2 No register clamping at $k = 2000$.** $k = 2000$ is 97.7% of the 12-bit signed range and $\hat s$ there agrees with $k = 1000$ to $5\times10^{-5}$. The ladder is linear over its whole usable range.

## 6. The pre-registered rule, and why it returned "unresolved"

R-O1 asked for $\hat s$ to sit within 3 SE of exactly one of $\{0.512, 0.500\}$. It sits within 3 SE of *neither*: 338 σ from one and 12.7 σ from the other. **The rule behaved correctly and the honest report is the number, not an adjudication** — the hypothesis set was too small, because it contained no entry for "0.5 modified by a per-axis gain".

This is worth recording as a methodological point for the manuscript's pre-registration discipline: a closed hypothesis set is only protective if it is *complete*. R6 (TN-14 §6) makes the same demand of $\{H_0 \ldots H_3\}$, and the same failure mode is available there.
